Predicates used: OnTable(x): block x is on the table. On(x, y): block x

the robotic arm is not holding any block. Holding(x): the robotic arm is
holding block x.

Initial state: I = { OnTable(A), OnTable(B), OnTable(C), OnTable(D),
Clear(A), Clear(B), Clear(C), Clear(D), ArmEmpty }

Goal state: G = {On(A, C), On(D, B)}

Operators:

1.  PICKUP(x) Preconditions: OnTable(x), Clear(x), ArmEmpty Effects:
    Holding(x), not OnTable(x), not ArmEmpty

2.  STACK(x, y) Preconditions: Holding(x), Clear(y) Effects: On(x, y),
    Clear(x), ArmEmpty, not Holding(x), not Clear(y)

Goal-stack plan:

Step 1: PICKUP(A) The arm picks up A from the table.

Step 2: STACK(A, C) A is placed on C. The condition On(A, C) is now
satisfied.

satisfied.

Clear(D), ArmEmpty }

QUESTION 2. KNOWLEDGE-BASED SYSTEM FOR RESEARCH FELLOWSHIP

(i) First-Order Logic representation of the four policies

Policy 1: Every student studies at least one course.

For every x, if x is a student, there is at least one y such that y is a
course and x studies y.

∀x [Student(x) → ∃y (Course(y) ∧ Studies(x, y))]

Policy 2: A student who studies a course and passes that same course is
qualified for research.

∀x∀y [(Student(x) ∧ Course(y) ∧ Studies(x, y) ∧ Passed(x, y)) →
Qualified(x)]

Policy 3: A qualified student who is supervised by a professor is
considered a researcher.

∀x∀y [(Professor(x) ∧ Supervises(x, y) ∧ Qualified(y)) → Researcher(y)]

Policy 4: Every researcher is eligible for a research fellowship.

∀x [Researcher(x) → Eligible(x)]

(ii) Meaning of the given statement

Given statement:

∀x (Student(x) → ∃y (Course(y) ∧ Studies(x, y)))

This statement says that every student studies at least one course. It
does not say that all students study one common course. The course can
be different for each student.

We also cannot conclude that every student studies AI. The given facts
say that Ravi studies AI and Anu studies ML. Therefore, the statement
only guarantees that each student studies at least one course; it does
not guarantee that the course is AI or that it is the same for everyone.

(iii) Deriving Eligible(Ravi)

Yes, Eligible(Ravi) can be derived from the given facts and policies.

Given facts relevant to Ravi: Student(Ravi) Course(AI) Studies(Ravi, AI)
Passed(Ravi, AI) Professor(DrSharma) Supervises(DrSharma, Ravi)

Step 1: Derive Qualified(Ravi)

Using Policy 2, Ravi is a student, AI is a course, Ravi studies AI, and
Ravi has passed AI. Therefore:

Qualified(Ravi)

Step 2: Derive Researcher(Ravi)

Using Policy 3, DrSharma is a professor, DrSharma supervises Ravi, and
Ravi is qualified. Therefore:

Researcher(Ravi)

Step 3: Derive Eligible(Ravi)

Using Policy 4, every researcher is eligible for a research fellowship.
Since Ravi is a researcher:

Eligible(Ravi)

Conclusion:

Eligible(Ravi) is successfully derived. The sequence is:

Qualified(Ravi) → Researcher(Ravi) → Eligible(Ravi)

This is a forward-chaining derivation because each rule is applied to
facts already available to produce a new fact.
